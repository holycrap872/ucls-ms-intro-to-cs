## Essential Questions

- How do AI generate art?
- How can we iteratively use AI to improve outcomes?

## Lesson Plan

In this lesson, students are asked to try and generate images via AI that match
a given, "target" image. During the lesson, they learn about the importance of
being specific with their language and the difficulty of communicating ideas to
computers. The image generation bit takes around 30 minutes (with more if
there's a good discussion/reflection).

### Actual Lesson

- Reflect
    - Have you used AI?
    - What do you know about AI art?
    - Iteratively using AI to improve
- Article on AI art (TODO)
    - 5m to read
    - 5m to discuss
- Explain goal
    - See if can create AI image that matches a real image
        - Reverse abstraction... getting more and more details
    - Show 4-5 prompts of me attempting to get closer to target image
    - What type of "art class" words can we use to describe things?
        - Be specific about what you see
        - "Light green triangular shapes in the foreground"
        - "Texture of finger painting"
        - "Shadow on neck that is blurred by foliage"
- Do "target image" as a class
    - Try and make Georgia O'Keeffe's "Lake George"
    - Have 3-4 students try and improve the prompt incrementally
        - Use language as if you were in art!
- Have to to create "target image" individually
    - Picture of kids sneaking out of house
    - Give around 7-10 minutes
- Reflect
    - What was easy?
    - What was hard?
    - Why did AI struggle to produce certain types of images
    - Discuss how easy it is to make disinformation of people sneaking **into** house
